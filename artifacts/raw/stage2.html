The columns, exactly

The Market Monitor is Pradeep Bonde's (Stockbee), from his blog, stockbee.blogspot.com. We count US stocks priced at $5 or more and trading at least $1 million a day on average, a session at a time.

  • Up 4% / Down 4%: closed 4% or more above (below) the previous close, on volume above the previous day's and at least 100,000 shares.
  • 5-day and 10-day ratio: the 4% breakouts over the last 5 (10) sessions divided by the 4% breakdowns over the same sessions.
  • Up / Down 25% in a quarter: the close is at least 25% above (below) the close 65 sessions earlier.
  • Up / Down 25% and 50% in a month: the same against the close 21 sessions earlier.
  • Up / Down 13% in 34 days: the same against the close 34 sessions earlier.
  • Above the 40-day average: the share of stocks with 40 sessions of history that closed above their 40-day average.

Reading the Market Monitor

What is the Stockbee Market Monitor?

A breadth table Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee) keeps to judge when the market favors momentum trades. Each row is one session: how many stocks broke out or down 4% on rising volume, how those counts balance over five and ten days, how many stocks have made big moves over a month and a quarter, and what share sits above its 40-day average. This page builds the same columns on US stocks after every close.

How do you read the 5-day and 10-day ratios?

Each is the number of 4% breakouts over the period divided by the number of 4% breakdowns. Readings well above 1 mean buyers are winning day after day, which is when breakouts tend to follow through; readings well below 1 mean sellers are in control. Bonde watches for the ratio turning after an extreme rather than for any single day.

What does the share above the 40-day average show?

How much of the market is in a short-term uptrend. It is the same idea as the T2108 reading Bonde quotes, which counts stocks above their 40-day average. Very low readings, around 20% or less, have tended to come near selling climaxes; very high ones mean the market is stretched.

Why are the counts smaller than on Stockbee's own sheet?

This page counts only stocks over our liquidity floor, so thin penny stocks that jump 4% on a few trades are left out. The direction and the turns of each column are what matter, and those carry over.

When does it update?

After every US close. The nightly run starts at around 5:30 pm ET, and each row is dated to its trading session.

End-of-day data for common stocks and ADRs on the NYSE, Nasdaq and NYSE American, computed after each US close and dated to the trading session.
